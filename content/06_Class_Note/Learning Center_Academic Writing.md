**Purpose**
Ask:
What do I want my audience to understand?
Why is it important that they understand this?
What do I want my audience to gain from my work?
How do I want my audience to feel after reading my work?

on an overall level, a section level, a paragraph level

Overall: making clear my proposed research is about combining computation and craft, through the material process of constructing an 8 bit computer. It is important because current computation design is too abstract to empower people. And I want to propose a new design principle that I am going to try on my own and document here (it is important!). 

Noting down clarity for yourself, to an audience, think of it as an elevator pitch, in a couple of sentences, quick 30 second of rundown, more specificity, exercise of discipline. Express what we want to say, in a short format, the more expansive the explanation is, the less settled it seems the writer is.

Scope
- extent, boundary, what aspect of this broad canvas are we looking at
	- lost depth
	- lost of context, too intensely focused
	- finding a balance
- feasibility, within timeline, the program
- choose your lens
	- How will you look at your topic?
	- Which aspects?
	- Who is involved or affected?
	- Where and when will you focus your discussion?
- think about the purpose
	- What do you want to prove, demonstrate?
	- What questions to answer?
	- What would you like your audience to understand?

Research Space
- establish research territory = broad topic
	- answer: why is this research area important?
	- ...has been extensively studied...
	- recent studies have focused on...
	- ...has become a major issue
	- find a foundation to build out your own work
- establish a niche
	- highlight gaps/problems/unclarity/addition that justify your work
	- Previous studies of ... have not examined..
	- Such studies are unsatisfactory because
	- One question that needs to be asked, however is...
	- Research on.. has mostly been restricted to...so...
- occupy the niche
	- how you are addressing the need objectives, questions, methods, announce findings (anticipated), indicate structure of your research process
	- how to map out your plan
	- goes back to so what? why?
