## Notes

General knowledge about plastics

- modern plastics comes from crude oil

```
			diesel
          /               
crude oil -- plastic (7%) -- thermoset
		  \               \
			Gasoline        thermo formable
			
```

- thermoset: Chemical process that sets into durable form, like polymer, monomer, it will be heated up until point of decomposition
- thermoformable: Recyclable. When heated up, it can be reformed into something else.

- thermoformable temperature state

```
acrylic
  stable range    thermoforming range           decomposition
<--------------><--------------------->*-------- - - -
    -30~40c                       glass point    

```

- PLA
	- made from corn/soybean, plant sources
	- poli-lactic-acid
	- "decomposable to organic compound"
	- but can only be decomposed in industrial bio-reactor, not something that can be recycled by anybody


- plastic grades
	- injection grade: High Melt Flow Index (MFI). This plastic is designed to become very liquid-like when melted.
	- vacuum-form grade: Low Melt Flow Index (MFI). This is often called "Extrusion Grade" because the sheets are originally made by an extruder.
	- they have different flow rate
	- While two parts might both be made of ABS, an **injection grade** ABS is fundamentally different from a **vacuum form grade** ABS.


- different plastics
![[different plastics.png]]

| **Code** | **Name (Abbreviation)** | **Characteristics**                              | **Common Uses**                            |
| -------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| **1**    | **PET (PETE)**          | Clear, strong, good gas barrier.                 | Water/soda bottles, peanut butter jars.    |
| **2**    | **HDPE**                | Opaque/milky, tough, chemically resistant.       | Milk jugs, detergent bottles, shampoo.     |
| **3**    | **PVC**                 | Rigid or flexible, durable, weather-resistant.   | Piping, credit cards, vinyl siding, wire.  |
| **4**    | **LDPE**                | Very flexible, soft, tough.                      | Squeeze bottles, grocery bags, cling wrap. |
| **5**    | **PP**                  | High melting point, tough, resistant to fatigue. | Yogurt cups, Tupperware, medicine bottles. |
| **6**    | **PS**                  | Rigid and brittle (solid) or light (foam).       | Plastic forks, CD cases, Styrofoam.        |
| **7**    | **Other**               | Catch-all for mixes (e.g., Polycarbonate, BPA).  | Baby bottles, 5-gallon water jugs, nylon.  |

- how to tell different plastics?

| **If it...**        | **And it...**                                          | **It is likely...** |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| **Sinks in Water**  | **Shatters** with a hammer                             | **PLA**             |
| **Floats in Water** | **Dents deeply** and feels waxy                        | **LDPE**            |
| **Floats in Water** | **Dents slightly** and is very rigid                   | **HDPE**            |
| **Floats in Water** | **Turns white** (cracks) at the hit point/Very springy | **PP**              |

If you are still stuck between HDPE and PP (since both float and both are tough):

- **The Scratch Test:** Try to scratch it with your fingernail. You can usually leave a faint mark on **LDPE** and sometimes **HDPE**. You almost never can on **PP** or **PLA**.
    
- **The Feel:** **HDPE** and **LDPE** feel "waxy" or "soapy" (like a candle). **PP** feels "plasticky" and grippy. **PLA** feels very hard, almost like a ceramic.
