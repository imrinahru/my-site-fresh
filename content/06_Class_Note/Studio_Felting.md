# Studio: Felting

Week1

wool
- raw fibre: lanolin, the grease, moisture on natural wool, don't want to use for felting
- roving, combed, smooth
- merino, smooth surface
- magnified, pine cone, with scales on the fibre, the scale can be opened or closed, by heating, it can be opened, then shrinked later
- the needle will tangle the wool to form the structure

Staple??
- Individual fibre is longer than the distance->no tear
- depending on the breed of sheep, animal, the length of fibre is different

Wet felt
Industrial felt, too thick and dense for needle felting


Color blending
- overlay
- tear
- overlay
- repeat
- do any amount of fibre

2D needle felting
- always stick back into a form 
- non dominant hand for stabilizing
- pen-shaped holder,

definition, and pulling in of the fibre happens as you stub up and down on the felt, works along
- Don't over needle early on if you want to blend, 
	- the denser, it becomes harder to add new layer
	- just a little, to secure the felt to surface
	- the felt will shrink into the direction you poked, diagonal can help with shaping
	- work along the line to define the edge
	- also twisting the fibre will help for defining edge
	- mind the shrinkage
	- periodically lift off from the form
	- dot detail, take a small piece of fibre, roll it in the finger and then felt it
	- If doing a shape, start from the middle, then draw in the edge
	- defined edge: pull the edge in using needle
	- fading edge, just let it blend into background
	- pinch test: see if individual fibre can be pulled away, you likely need to do a bit more needling.
	- But also, even if you want that loose effect, make sure one end is secured in
	- less is more
	- felting is irreversible, can't take away

3D
- primitive shape
- using kebab skewer to do a long stick
- can use multiple strips to build up the stick
- egg ball, head,
- rectangular, square: torso, back..the thicker the yarn, the bigger the sh
- disc
- triangle
- see the thing in some primitive shapes
- use small thin chunks to start with, 
- wrap around
- make use of the loose fibre
- then as building up, building the density, quite hard
- Towards the end, you can add more detail by shaping the contour
