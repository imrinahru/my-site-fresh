## Theme 1: Temporality and Craft

It is understood that craft is defined less by its outcome than by its materials and processes. Digital tools have a significant impact on the _tempo_ of making (e.g., rapid iteration versus slow material learning).

From the perspective of temporality, the essay explores how craft has adapted to the temporal conditions of each generation, and how digital tools have reshaped craft by introducing a new temporality. What is gained, and what is lost? And how might we reconcile the temporality of nature with the fast-paced modes of making toward which we are shifting?


## Theme 2: Care and Repair

When comparing the Mexican market and the project carried out at the University of Tasmania, one is struck by the different cultural perspectives underlying each practice. One is communal, where no single hero is named, but rather reflects a continuing heritage and tradition; the other is individual (or at most familial), where a consumer and a creator are brought together for a particular occasion.

The essay compares these two instances, which originate from different cultural narratives, with an equal and balanced approach. Both have contributed to discussions of care and repair within their respective social contexts. More importantly, the essay seeks to incite a cross-cultural dialogue on what can be adopted and adapted from each side, and how these insights might be applied within their own contexts. The essay positions itself as a site for questioning singular cultural narratives, incorporating a decolonizing perspective and encouraging cross-pollination.
