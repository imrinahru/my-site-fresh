# TA Preparation & Teaching Framework

## 1. Role of a TA

TAs are not responsible for introducing **new content**.  
Your role is to **facilitate learning** — helping students engage deeply with lecture materials and assigned readings.

Think of your tutorial as a **study group**, where students:

- Develop critical thinking skills
    
- Explore course material in depth
    
- Prepare for assignments and tests
    
- Support one another through shared learning strategies and community building
    

### Modes of Facilitation

**Synchronous (live tutorials):**

- Lead discussions on lecture content
    
- Summarize and clarify key ideas
    
- Prompt students to explain or apply specific readings/themes
    

**Asynchronous (online engagement):**

- Share reading prompts or reflective questions
    
- Post-lecture surveys to gauge understanding
    
- Provide feedback on discussion boards
    
- Curate extra resources or clarifications

## 2. Workload & Administrative Planning

### Workload Plan

- Estimate time for:
    
    - Grading and feedback
        
    - Tutorial preparation (reviewing materials, creating activities/resources)
        
    - Managing discussion boards or online components
        

### Check-ins & Coordination

- **Canvas / Teams:**  
    Confirm your responsibilities:
    
    - Setting up calendar invites for tutorials?
        
    - Managing discussion boards or announcements?
        
    - Maintaining a tutorial channel in Teams?
        
- **Accommodations:**
    
    - You may not automatically receive SAS accommodation emails.
        
    - Ask the instructor if there are students with accommodations relevant to your TA role.
        
- **Resources & Materials:**
    
    - Clarify access to lecture notes and readings.
        
    - Confirm whether any handouts or guides you create require faculty approval before sharing.
        
- **Grading:**
    
    - Determine whether grading is shared or independent.
        
    - Clarify:
        
        - Turnaround time for grades
            
        - Who inputs grades into Canvas
            
        - Whether faculty approval is needed before releasing grades
            
    - Confirm how to handle grade complaints (process/formality).
        
- **Issues & Escalation:**
    
    - Procedure for notifying faculty if you must cancel a tutorial
        
    - Boundaries for handling extensions, conflicts, or distressed students
        
    - Protocol for suspected plagiarism or re-mark requests
        
- **Communication & Check-ins:**
    
    - Establish frequency and method of check-ins with faculty
        
    - Confirm preferred form of address (Dr., Prof., First name)
        

---

## 3. Professional Practice as TA

- Use formal tone in emails; listen carefully and respond thoughtfully in conversations.
    
- Reinforce lecture content through:
    
    - In-class tutorial activities (typically in the latter 30 minutes)
        
    - Experiential and discussion-based exercises
        
    - Short summaries or mini-presentations
        
- Maintain consistency and responsiveness — ensure students feel heard.
    
- Use repetition to reinforce learning; familiarity builds confidence.
    

### Workflow Summary

| Stage            | Focus                                                          | Tools            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Before Class** | Meet with instructor, align objectives, prep materials         | Teams channel    |
| **In Class**     | Take notes, observe engagement, facilitate group discussion    | Lecture/tutorial |
| **After Class**  | Meet or message students, check-in online, summarize takeaways | Canvas, email    |

# Business & Social Innovation

## Theme / Topic

**Main Topic:** Welcome / Introduction  
**Key Reading:** Garvin et al. (2008) _Is Yours a Learning Organization?_

### Instructor Highlights

**Course Purpose:**  
To equip emerging artists and designers with the ability to:

- Develop **alternative business models** and **value propositions**
    
- Understand intersections of **creativity, business, and social impact**
    
- Work collaboratively and assess organizational structures
    

**Core Themes:**

- Social vs. traditional (capitalist) economy
    
- Sustainability and leadership in creative industries
    
- Business models oriented toward social innovation and creative ethics
    

**Guiding Questions:**

- Can we “make money and do good” simultaneously?
    
- What alternative business models exist for the 21st century creative economy?
    

**Frameworks Introduced:**

- **Business Model Canvas (BMC)**
    
- **SWOT Analysis** (strategic mapping)
    
- **STEEPLE Analysis** (environmental scanning)

## Instructor Notes & Reflections

> **Key Distinction:**  
> All businesses have social dimensions, but **not all are social enterprises.**  
> Social enterprises intentionally prioritize **societal and environmental value** over private profit.

> **Student Concerns:**  
> Many feel anxious about balancing financial success and social good. Address this openly.

> **Social Innovation Definition (Phills et al., 2008):**  
> A _novel solution to a social problem_ that primarily benefits society rather than individuals.

> **Community of Inquiry (COI):**  
> Effective learning occurs where **social**, **cognitive**, and **teaching presence** intersect.

**Class Activities:**

- Group collaboration and role-play around creative business ideas
    
- Reflect on teamwork as social innovation: “Followship” and supportive leadership

# Week 2 – Leadership & Learning Organizations

## Theme / Topic

**Main Topic:** Leadership, Team Development, and Social Enterprise  
**Reading:** Garvin et al. (2008) _Is Yours a Learning Organization?_  
**Focus:** The “Three Building Blocks” of a learning organization

### Key Concepts

1. **Supportive Learning Environment**
    
    - Psychological safety
        
    - Valuing differences
        
    - Time for reflection
        
2. **Concrete Learning Processes**
    
    - Experimentation, analysis, education, and knowledge sharing
        
3. **Leadership that Reinforces Learning**
    
    - Modeling curiosity and reflection
        
    - Encouraging openness and ownership
        

**Supplementary Frameworks:**

- **BMC (Business Model Canvas)** — understanding value creation
    
- **SWOT vs. SWOC Analysis** — identifying internal/external factors
    
- **STEEPLE Analysis** — broader socio-environmental context
    
- **Tuckman’s Team Development Model** — forming, storming, norming, performing
    

---

## Case Examples

- [StopGap Foundation](https://stopgap.ca/) — accessible design with social and environmental impact
    
- [TNG Community To](https://tngcommunityto.org/About-Us/Our-Story/St-Stephen-s-Community-House) — community-centered innovation
    

---

## Instructor Highlights

- “Go slow to go far.” Encourage students to relax and reflect.
    
- Evaluate social enterprises through the **triple bottom line**: _social, economic, environmental_.
    
- Use **local, boutique-style creative businesses** for examples.
    
- Discuss **IKEA** as contrast — global profit concentration vs. community reinvestment.
    

> **Observation:** Few students are familiar with the SDGs — revisit in tutorial.

## Readings Summary (for reference)

|Author|Key Insight|
|---|---|
|**Garvin et al. (2008)**|Three elements of learning organizations: environment, processes, leadership|
|**Parsley (2014)**|Leadership as social influence guiding relationships toward shared aims|
|**Reynolds & Lewis (2016)**|Cognitive diversity enhances problem-solving; recruit for difference|
|**Eisenhardt et al. (1997)**|Productive conflict through fact-based debate, humor, and balanced power|
|**Gino & Staats (2015)**|Organizational learning blocked by biases (success, action, fitting in, experts)|
|**Scannell (2010)**|Conflict resolution: identify, communicate, resolve, follow-up|

**Main Takeaways:**

- Students need grounding in real-world examples of learning organizations
    
- Many relate to creative leadership through collaboration and curiosity
    
- Future tutorials should balance conceptual grounding and lived experience
