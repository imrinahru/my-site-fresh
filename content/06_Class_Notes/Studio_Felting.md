**Fibre blending with the drum carder**
- The drum carder is great for blending larger quantities of wool fibre.
- Lay out roving in layers on the feeder bed and turn the handle clockwise to comb the fibre onto the metal teeth.
- You can add more fibre, but ensure the fibre doesn't exceed the height of the metal teeth.
- Remove the wool batt using a knitting needle or supplied stick along the groove in the drum. Pull upwards to split the fibres and carefully pull the batt away from the drum.
- Re-blend multiple times for homogenous colour; blend fewer times for mottled or streaked effects.

## Wet Felting: Basic Flat Sheets

### Materials

- Felting Mat (Sushi mats, bamboo blind, bubble wrap, shelf liners)
- Wool Fleece
- Hot water
- Sponges
- Fine netting/mesh
- Cups/bowls
- Towels and plastic sheeting to cover the workspace
- Olive Oil Soap Gel
- Vinegar rinse


### Setting Up the Workspace

1. If you need to protect the workspace from water, cover with a plastic sheet.
2. Lay out a towel on the workspace, followed by your felting mat.
3. Keep boiled water in a bowl or jug next to you. Make sure the water is hot, but not too hot to touch with your bare hands. Dilute boiled water from the kettle with water from the tap.
4. Pour a small amount of olive oil soap gel into a small bowl.


### Summary

1. layout the fibres in layers. Different colours and compositions can be laid out at this stage; there is a lot of potential for layering and colour blocking.
2. embellish at this stage while it's dry. Natural materials like wool, silk and cotton yarn or fabric work well as embellishments. Smoother materials like wire and polyester may be more difficult to embed in the fabric and will require layers of felt on top to secure the embellishments
3. Cover with net, soak with hot water & soap, work surface
4. Pinch test: pre-felt stage complete
5. Fulling stage: Roll to full/shrink the fabric. This gives the cloth strength and durability. An optional second stage in fulling is scrunching and throwing the felt sheet.

Felt shrinks in the direction of rolling, so reorient by 90˚ after every round of rolls. 50 rolls in each direction is a good starting
point.

6. Rinse in water to remove excess soap and neutralise with a vinegar dip to finish.


### Detailed instructions

#### Laying Out and Wetting the Fibre

1. Working with a wool sliver, tear off about 30 cm and gently pull and separate the fibres. Working carefully to separate the fibres, divide the sliver into 4 pieces. Take each of the 4 pieces, and pull it apart in lengths as long as your hand. These will be made into the shingles for felting.
2. Hold the small length in your dominant hand and anchor the opposite end under your other hand, using the flat of your forefinger. Pull out the fibre, creating a very thin, even shingle of wool. Gently pat this down against the mat before making the next shingle.
3. Lay out a layer of small thin shingles on the mat, making sure that all the fibres are aligned and going in the same horizontal direction. Overlap the thin tops and bottoms of the shingles. Pat down each shingle as you move on to the next. Check that the layer is even and free of holes or thin areas. You should be able to see through this first layer.
4. Following the method outlined in step 3, lay down a second layer of wool shingles in the opposite direction. Follow this with a third and fourth layer, each time laying the fibres in the opposite direction to the layer below. You will have four layers. **This is** **a** **wool batt**.
5. Gently tuck in any wispy bits of wool that stick out beyond the edges of the wool batt, taking care not to fold them under, but just to tuck them in.
6. Carefully lay a piece of netting over the fibres. Holding the wool batt with one hand, use the other hand to sprinkle the wetting mixture over your hand . Press down for a few moments to allow the wool to absorb the mixture. Move your hand, and continue to add more wetting mixture until the entire batt is saturated. You can also use a sponge to gently squeeze water over the surface and press the fibres down to saturate them. Take care not to add too much water, but the wool should be fully saturated, flat and will stick to the mat with no air bubbles.  
    _****This is the point where you coulc add prefelt designs or attach cords/ropes or other additions.**_ 

#### Soft Felting Process [Fibres Entangling]

1. In this process, your hands are moving over the felt on a layer of soap rather than directly on the wool. Rub your palms with the soap gel mix and press down on the wet felt. Move your hands over the entire surface of the wool, adding more soap if the fibres stick to you and alternating the pressing with a very gentle circular rubbing using the flat of your hand. Soap and water will come out of the felt as the fibres mesh. Continue with these motions until the felt comes together and feels fabric-like. Do a test by running your fingertips across the felted surface; the fibres should remain intact. You can now remove the netting.
2. Next use a second piece of bubblewrap or a shelf liner laid on the top of the wool batt, with the bubbles/ridges of the mat facing down towards the fibre. Wet and soap your hands further to reduce friction, and gently rub the top of the plastic to softly felt the wool until it feels firmer. Work over the entire surface, especially the edges. Check the felt periodically and add more soap gel mix if it is getting dry.
3. **Pinch Test:** Test the felt by pinching the top layer of fibres on the surface. If the fabric remains intact, you can go on to the next step. If it pulls apart, continue rubbing until the fabric is harder.

#### Fulling the Felt [Shrinking Stage]

1. Now that the felt is sturdier, you can apply more agitation. There are several ways to full the felt. A good starting point is to roll it, then throw it.
2. **Rolling:** Remove the top layer of bubblewrap and tightly roll up the felt along with the felting mat and towel underneath. Roll the long bundle back and forth, trying to be as consistent as possible with your pressure across the bundle. To start, roll 30 times, unroll it, move the felt 90  
    degrees and begin the process again. Continue in this manner.
3. Lay the felt out flat again and measure it (if working to measurements). If you need to felt it more, rub the piece firmly across the ridges on the mat. Rub hard, keeping enough soapy water in the fabric to prevent pilling. If the edges of the felt are uneven, you can “spot felt” by rubbing the specific areas that need shrinking. **Felt shrinks fastest in the direction it is rubbed.**
4. **Throwing:** An optional extra step is to throw the felt to encourage more shrinkage. Remove the felt from the bubble wrap, scrunch tit up and throw the wet felt down onto a low table or a clean sink; it should be wet enough to make a loud thump when thrown. Add more wetting liquid if the felt becomes dry.

#### Finishing the Felt

1. When the felt is slightly larger than the desired finished measurements, and feels firm and well felted to the touch, rinse all the soap out under a running tap. Dip the finished felt in vinegar bath, squeeze out and lay flat to dry. The felt may be steam ironed to flatten it before drying. Allow to dry.

#### Prefelt for Inlay

Prefelt is simply lightly felted wool. It can be cut with scissors into shapes (wet or dry) and placed on the wet felt just before you start rubbing and felting the surface to create a collaged surface. Once you have placed the pieces of prefelt, lightly rub with the flat of your hand for about 2-3 minutes. Once the prefelt adheres to the fibres it should not be moved but simply felted into place.

Olive Oil Soap

100% pure olive oil bar soap has a lower pH level (8-9) than other bar soaps and detergents (10-12), making it ideal for wet felt making. The lower pH value of olive oil soap is closer to that of wool (5) and therefore it reacts better with the fibre to entangle and there is less loss of lanolin. Olive oil soap has a good friction rate with fleece roving for making felt and washes out of wool very easily. It is also easier on your hands than other soaps.

Olive oil soap is sold in bar form. This can be made into a soap gel that you can dilute with water to create a wetting liquid for use in your wet felt making. Most health food stores carry olive oil soap.

In the studio we use a 10% olive oil soap solution which is stored in the kitchen area in a tall square plastic jug. This solution is a bar soap that is grated and dissolved with water to make a gel that is then diluted to 10% for a working soap solution. 

_Note: the olive oil soap used in the studio is ‘Kiss my face’ brand - 100% pure olive oil bar soap and it is unscented. We do not encourage the use of strongly scented products in the studio._ 

Olive Oil Soap Gel Recipe (studio staff make this for us!)

_Recipe sourced from felt artist and author Chad Alice Hagan_ 

120g 100% Olive Oil Soap Bar

500ml Boiling Water

1. Grate bar soap or chop into small pieces. 
2. Boil water and pour over soap pieces, mix well with a whisk or fork.
3. Cover and leave it to set for 6-10 hours. 
4. Mix well again after the setting period, it should be a medium thick liquid (like maple syrup) and uniform in colour when ready for use. 
5. Store airtight on a cool shelf for up to 12 months. Keep stored under constant temperature of 15-30˚C. 

Olive Oil Soap 10% solution (studio staff will refill as needed): 

1. In the designated square plastic jug, measure 10% soap gel and add 90% water (eg. 100ml soap gel to 900ml room temp water) 
2. Close the lid tightly and shake jug **lightly** to combine.
3. Shelf store in kitchen area, refill as needed. 

Vinegar Rinse

Once felting is complete, it is a good idea to give your felt a final vinegar rinse to return the pH to 4.9 and help close the fibres.

The rinse proportions are **1 litre cool water to 1.5 teaspoons of white vinegar.**

When felting is complete, rinse the felt in this mix, squeeze out excess, rinse in clean water and dry.

## Wet Felting Using a Resist

Resist felting is a technique that allows you to create 3D hollow forms. A smooth, waterproof, ‘unfeltable’ material is placed between layers of roving while the fibres are being laid out. This could be an acetate or plastic sheet, sheet foam packing material or a piece of craft foam. The resist can be completely concealed by fibre (good for making vase-type forms) or poke out from the fibre (as in making a pocket for a felted garment).
The resist is sandwiched inside the fibre and remains there for the felting stage. It is then removed (if the resist is concealed, the felt is cut), and the fulling stage is carefully executed in a 3-dimensional manner, working from the inside and outside of the form at once.

### Materials

- Felting Mat (Sushi mats, bamboo blind, bubble wrap, shelf liners)
- Wool Fleece
- Embellishment Materials (e.g. wool, cotton or silk yarns and loose fibres, sheer or openweave fabrics, lace, angelica fibre, netting, pre-felt shapes)
- Plastic or foam sheet, cut to desired shape (e.g. acetate, craft foam)
- Hot water
- Sponges
- Fine netting/mesh
- Cups/bowls
- Towels and plastic sheeting to cover the workspace
- Olive Oil Soap Gel
- Vinegar rinse (1L water: 1.5 tbsp vinegar)

### Setting Up the Workspace

1. If you need to protect the workspace from water, cover with a plastic sheet.
2. Lay out a towel on the workspace, followed by your felting mat.
3. Keep boiled water in a bowl or jug next to you. Make sure the water is hot, but not too hot to touch with your bare hands. Dilute boiled water from the kettle with water from the tap.
4. Pour a small amount of olive oil soap gel into a small bowl.  

### Laying Out and Wetting the Fibre

1. Decide on the form you want to make, and cut a piece of plastic/foam sheet to size. Lay this on your work surface.
2. Divide the wool into two piles; one for the front and one for the back; divide each of these into four piles for the four layers of wool shingles. With the plastic resist pattern on the felting mat, lay out the first side of the shape, using four layers of wool, each layer perpendicular to the last. Allow the edges of the wool batt to extend about 2 – 3 cm (1-1 ½”) beyond the edge of the resist; these are the seam allowances. 
    - _Get creative with colour and embellishment here! Consider layering different colours of roving on the top and bottom, blending roving together to create mottled or streaked effects, and laying embellishment materials on the surface._
    - _The roving that lays directly next to the resist template will be the inside of the vessel._
3. Pat down the batt, lay a sheet of netting over the top and then flip the whole batt + pattern over. Even out the extended wool of the seam allowance. Remove the resist and place the batt and netting aside.
4. Repeat to make the second side of the piece, but after flipping the batt over, remove the plastic resist and leave the batt on the mat. Gently wet the batt with wetting mix about 4 cm (1 ½”) inside the edges of the batt. Keep the wool outside this line dry (seam wool), but soak the inner area. Press down on the wet area until the wool absorbs the water and sticks to the felting mat. Replace the resist and smooth.
5. To retain an open edge on the form, pull back the plastic form on the desired edge and fold the dry wool over to the water line. Replace the plastic and press down to saturate.
6. Fold all the dry seam edges over the plastic pattern. Wet out with hot water. Take the other wool batt and place it exactly on top of the wet wool batt, keeping the netting on top of the batt and carefully matching up the edges on all four sides. Wet out the centre of the dry batt until you feel the edge of the plastic resist under the wool. As before, keep the wool dry beyond the edge of the form.
7. Using the netting to help you, pick up the entire three-layer piece at the top corners and flip it over. Lift high so dry wool areas do not fold under.
8. If you are retaining an opening, fold it back on this side as described in step 4. After replacing the top pattern and wet wool, press the opening edge to wet it out.
9. Fold over all the dry wool and press down to wet it out. This is the last moment you have to add any extra surface embellishments.

### Soft Felting the Batt [Fibres Entangling]

1. Rub your palms with the soap gel mix and press down on the wet felt. Move your hands over the entire surface of the wool and netting, adding more soap if the fibres stick to you and alternating the pressing with a very gentle circular rubbing using the flat of your hand. Turn the piece over and continue rubbing on the other side. Soap and water will come out of the felt as the fibres mesh. Continue with these motions until the felt comes together and feels fabric-like.
2. Gently run your fingertips across the felted surface; the fibres should remain intact. You can now remove the netting. If not, rub a little more.

### Removing the Resist

1. Remove the resist by cutting a slit/hole at an appropriate position, and start felting the piece from both inside and out, with one hand inside the piece. Gently open out the seam area and felt flat the ridge that may have formed. Continue to felt the piece, holding one hand on the inside of the piece and rubbing the felt between your open hands, moving consistently over the entire surface. 
2. **Pinch Test:** Test the felt by pinching the top layer of fibres on the surface. If the fibres pull up in a single layer, you can go on to the next step. If fibres pull away from the surface, continue rubbing until the fabric is harder.

### Fulling the Felt [Shrinking Stage]

1. Continue rubbing harder to start shrinking the felt. Rub the piece firmly across the ridges on the mat, using enough soapy water in the fabric to prevent pilling. If the edges of the felt are uneven, you can “spot felt” by rubbing the specific areas that need shrinking. **Felt shrinks fastest in the direction it is rubbed.**
2. Then throw the felt to encourage more shrinkage. Remove the felt from the bubble wrap and throw the wet felt down onto a low table or a clean sink; it should be wet enough to make a loud thump when thrown. Add more wetting liquid if the felt becomes dry.

### Finishing the Felt

1. Finish the felt by throwing it as described in step 10. Once the desired size is obtained, rinse the piece out in cool or hot water and then in a vinegar rinse. Leave to dry.

## Wet Felted Balls

1. Prepare your roving by loosening the fibres lengthways and sideways before beginning to shape into a ball.
2. There are a few approaches to forming a ball with dry fibre:
    1. Take a fist-size amount of carded (combed) wool and bunch it into a tight ball shape. Try to minimise folds or loose fibres.
    2. Take a long sliver of wool roving (~30cm), tie the first half of an overhand knot at one end and pull until the knot feels firm, then take the loose ends of roving and wrap it around the knot in a criss-cross, figure eight motion, turning the ball as you go. Smooth the fibre onto the knot firmly; you want all the layers to be close together without being too tight. Try to minimise folds or loose fibres.
    3. Take a long sliver of wool roving (~30cm) and position it vertically in front of you on your workstation. Start rolling up the sliver, starting with the loose fibres closest to you. Don't roll straight (this would create a cylindrical shape); roll the bottom right to the middle, then the left to the middle, and keep alternating and rotating the fibre as you go to create a ball shape. Try to minimise folds or loose fibres.
3. Once you have the ball core, you can add extra layers of fibre to increase the size or add surface embellishment. Build up the layers carefully and firmly, always rotating the ball in your hands as you lay on new layers of roving. Remember, you want a smooth surface, without folds or gaps between each layer of fibre. 
4. Keep building up layers until your ball reaches the desired size. The final size of the ball can be estimated by squeezing the fibre between your fingers.
5. Dip the ball into the hot water, thoroughly wetting it by squeezing several times to saturate. Squeeze out excess water and apply plenty of soap to your hands and the ball.
6. This is the last opportunity to add surface embellishment before rolling. Dry your hands, and apply finger-length slivers of wool or yarns to the surface, tightly wrapping them around the ball. Dip or gently sprinkle the ball to saturate the new materials.
7. Once all layers are added to the ball and it is fully wetted out with the fibres starting to hold together, begin to gently roll the ball between the palms of your hands, working slowly and carefully. Use plenty of soap mix. As the ball becomes sturdier, increase the pressure. As you continue to roll, the ball will become smaller and tighter as the fibres become more densely felted together.
8. When the felt ball is hard and has shrunk to the desired size, rinse out the soap under hot water and then give it a few squeezes in the vinegar rinse. Squeeze out as much liquid as you can, re-roll the ball back into shape and leave to dry.

### Ball Variations:

- Layer different colours on the ball and cut in half when finished to show the layers
- Add surface details before you begin felting
- Felt over a styrofoam ball and cut open to create bowls or vessels

## Wet Felted Ropes

1. Take desired length of wool sliver at desired thickness; the finished thickness can be determined by tightly twisting the wool sliver between your fingers. Submerge the wool sliver in the hot water to saturate the fibres. Gently squeeze out the excess liquid and lay out on the felting mat.
2. Apply plenty of olive oil soap to your hands and lightly roll the sliver on your felting mat until the wool takes on a rope-like shape. 
3. Using your fingers spread open and working firmly over the surface of the rope, gently roll back and forth on your felting mat until you feel the rope becoming firmer. Once the rope is formed, roll faster and harder until the rope reaches the desired firmness. 
4. Rinse the rope under hot water, squeeze it in vinegar rinse, rinse with water and let dry.

### Rope Variations:

- If you plan on attaching the rope to another piece of felt, leave a fluffy end of unfelted wool that can be felted into another piece.
- A loop can be made by leaving both ends unfelted and then attaching these ends to another piece.
- You can also play with colour variations: making striped ropes, twisting two colours together, or braiding three or more.
- Ropes made up of coloured layers can be cut into multi-coloured pieces that are like felt beads.
