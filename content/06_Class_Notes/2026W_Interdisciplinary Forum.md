# Artist Talk 1 — Caroline Seck Langill

## _Tracking the Lively Object_

### Overview

This talk explored how **media art and technological systems produce lifelike behaviours**. Rather than representing life visually, artists can create systems where objects **act, react, and interact**, producing the sensation of liveliness.

The discussion drew from media art history, philosophy, and curatorial practices to rethink how objects and technologies participate in artistic systems.

---

# Key Concepts

## Behaviour vs Representation

Media artworks often create **behavioural systems** rather than static representations.

Example:

- Norman White — _The Helpless Robot_
    

The robot appears emotionally dependent on viewers' attention. When ignored, it behaves as if distressed.

**Important idea**

> The feeling of “life” can emerge from behaviour rather than visual resemblance.

Related artist:

- Doug Back
    

These artists design **systems that generate responses**, giving artworks their own operational logic.

---

## Whole-Body Interaction

Reference:

- Martha Todd — _The Thinking Body_ (1937)
    

The body participates in cognition. Interactive artworks can therefore engage:

- movement
    
- perception
    
- sensory response
    

rather than purely visual interpretation.

---

## Glitches and Instability

Reference:

- Andreas Broeckmann — machine aesthetics
    

Technological systems often appear more “alive” when they contain:

- glitches
    
- unpredictability
    
- instability
    

Perfectly controlled systems feel mechanical, while **imperfect systems produce vitality**.

---

# Philosophical Frameworks

## Posthumanism

Key theorist:

- N. Katherine Hayles
    
- _How We Became Posthuman_
    

Hayles critiques how digital culture often prioritizes **information over material embodiment**.

Example assumptions she challenges:

- mind vs body
    
- software vs hardware
    
- information vs matter
    

The talk emphasized **reintroducing materiality into discussions of digital media**.

---

## Actor-Network Theory

Associated with:

- Bruno Latour
    

Key concept: **actants**

An actant can be:

- human
    
- object
    
- machine
    
- system
    

Anything capable of **producing effects in a network**.

Agency emerges from **performance and interaction**, not simply intention.

---

## Vital Materialism

Key theorist:

- Jane Bennett
    

Her theory proposes that matter contains **energetic vitality** rather than being inert.

Objects carry:

- histories
    
- forces
    
- material agency
    

This perspective has also been critiqued for echoing ideas already present in **Indigenous philosophies of living matter**.

---

# Media Art Context

## Analog → Digital Transition

Key technological shift:

**1970–1991**

This period saw the transition from:

- analog electronics
    
- early computing systems
    
- digital networks
    
- emergence of the **World Wide Web**
    

These developments shaped early **interactive and media art practices**.

---

## Early Experiments in Art + Technology

Example:

- 9 Evenings: Theatre and Engineering
    

Artists such as John Cage collaborated with engineers to explore **technological performance systems**.

---

## Permeable Media Systems

Different media forms allow different levels of **interaction and openness**.

Closed systems

- painting
    
- film
    

More permeable systems

- interactive installations
    
- generative systems
    
- VR environments
    

These allow external influences to alter the artwork’s behaviour.

---

## Example of Interactive Wearable Art

- Go-Go Gloves by Kate Hartman
    

Wearable gloves that express the wearer’s enthusiasm through visible gestures.

Demonstrates:

- emotional interface
    
- wearable computing
    
- body–technology interaction

# Artist Talk 2 — Swapnaa Tamhane

## Practice Overview

Tamhane is an artist and designer whose work engages with:

- textile traditions
    
- paper making
    
- drawing
    
- craft knowledge
    
- postcolonial histories
    

Her practice connects **material processes with cultural memory**.

---

# Core Themes

## Drawing vs Craft

A key question raised:

How does **drawing in art** differ from **drawing in craft traditions**?

In craft contexts, drawing often functions as:

- instruction
    
- pattern
    
- structural design
    

rather than purely expressive imagery.

Tamhane emphasized:

> Art-making is a **long-term practice**, not simply a finished product.

---

## Material Genealogies

Example material: **cotton**

Cotton connects several processes:

- handmade cotton paper
    
- textile weaving
    
- block printing
    
- embroidery traditions
    

It also reflects historical relationships between:

- craft labor
    
- colonial trade
    
- industrial production.
    

---

## Craft Techniques Referenced

Examples discussed in the talk:

- block printing on cloth
    
- appliqué lettering
    
- mirror embroidery
    

Specific traditions:

- **Lippan Kaam** (mirror surface decoration)
    
- **Pakko embroidery**
    

These techniques emphasize **manual skill and embodied knowledge**.

---

## Craft and Anti-Colonial Politics

Reference:

- Mahatma Gandhi
    

Gandhi promoted **Khadi textiles** as a form of resistance to British industrial production.

Craft became both:

- cultural practice
    
- political statement.
    

---

## Learning from Artisans

Tamhane described working closely with artisan communities.

Important philosophy:

- knowledge sharing
    
- openness in technique
    
- rejection of gatekeeping.
    

Insight from artisans:

> When knowledge circulates freely, communities become richer.

---

## Anchoring Artistic Practice

Artists often move across different materials and disciplines.

However, over time it becomes important to identify **an anchoring point**.

For Tamhane, that anchor is **cotton**, which continually reappears in her work across different media.

Her process often involves:

exploration → distance → return to foundational material.

- technological systems (Langill)
    
- craft materials (Tamhane)
    

---

### 2. Knowledge Systems

Art practices emerge through networks of:

- technology
    
- artisans
    
- materials

---

### 3. Re-grounding in Materiality

In response to increasing digitalization, many artists return to **physical materials and craft knowledge**.
